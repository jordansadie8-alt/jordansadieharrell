## Definition
A system where human attention, not money or goods, is the scarcest and most valuable commodity. In this economy, products are designed not to be "useful," but to be "captivating," competing aggressively to harvest the limited hours of focus a human being possesses.

## Meaning (expanded)
Coined by [Nobel laureate Herbert Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_A._Simon), the concept relies on the principle that "a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention." Because digital content is effectively infinite but human time is strictly finite, platforms are locked in a zero-sum arms race.

* **The Extraction:** To win, platforms must deploy increasingly aggressive psychological tactics (variable rewards, outrage, sexual stimuli) to "frack" for attention.
* **The Commodity:** This transforms the user from a "customer" into the "product." The user's gaze is captured, bundled, and sold to advertisers.
* **The Asymmetry:** The user is often fighting against a supercomputer aimed at their amygdala. The "race to the bottom of the brain stem" refers to the need for apps to trigger more primal instincts (fear, lust, anger) to override higher-level reasoning.

## Archetypal Figures & Case Studies

> [!abstract] The Growth Hacker (The Engineer):
> **Optimization over Ethics.** The Growth Hacker's metric is "Time on Site" or "Daily Active Users." They tweak the colour of buttons, the timing of notifications, and the speed of the scroll to maximize these numbers. They do not ask *why* the user is scrolling, only *that* they are scrolling.
> 
> **Lesson:** When metrics are divorced from morality, addiction is categorized as success.

> [!abstract] The Binge Watcher (The Passive Subject):
> **Loss of Stopping Cues.** This user intends to watch one episode or read one tweet. Three hours later, they "wake up." The system removed all natural stopping cues (the end of a chapter, the closing of a book), replacing them with "Autoplay" and "Infinite Scroll" to keep the subject in a consumption trance. 
>
> **Lesson:** Technology that removes friction often removes agency.

> [!abstract] The Slot Machine (The Interface):
> **Intermittent Variable Rewards.** The "Pull-to-Refresh" mechanic is consciously designed to mimic a slot machine. The user pulls (action) to see if they got a reward (a like, a new post, a match). Because the reward is unpredictable, the behavior becomes compulsive.
> 
> **Lesson:** Unpredictability creates dependency.

## The Narrative Arc (The Hook Cycle)

1. **The Trigger (External/Internal):** The phone buzzes (external) or the user feels a moment of boredom/anxiety (internal). The economy has trained the user to soothe this micro-pain with a screen.
    
2. **The Action (The Frictionless Entry):** The user unlocks the phone. The apps are designed to load instantly with high-stimulation content immediately visible. There is no barrier to entry.
    
3. **The Variable Reward (The Hunt):** The user scrolls. Most content is boring, but occasionally they find something funny or validating. This "hit" of dopamine keeps them pulling the lever.
    
4. **The Investment (The Lock-in):** The user posts, comments, or curates their profile. This "labor" increases their psychological commitment to the platform, making it harder to leave.
    

## Signs / markers

- **The "Free" Trap:** If a service is free, the user is not the customer; they are the product being sold.
    
- **Infinite Scroll:** The removal of "stopping cues" (like page numbers) to induce a flow state where the user loses track of time.
    
- **Phantom Vibration Syndrome:** The physical sensation that a phone is buzzing when it isn't—evidence that the nervous system has become hyper-vigilant to the economy's demands.
    
- **Erosion of Deep Work:** The inability to sit with a complex task for more than 15 minutes without seeking a digital distraction.
    

## Examples  

- **Netflix vs. Sleep:** The CEO of Netflix famously stated that their biggest competitor is not HBO, but "sleep." This frames the business goal as biological colonization.
    
- **YouTube Autoplay:** Defaulting to play the next video automatically exploits the user's passive nature, stripping the moment of choice.
    
- **Clickbait:** Headlines written specifically to trigger the "information gap" curiosity instinct, prioritizing the click over the accuracy or quality of the content.
    

## Contrasts

- **Opposite:** **[[The Intention Economy]]** (A system where the user's specific intent drives the transaction, rather than the platform's manipulation of their attention).
    
- **Opposite:** **[[Deep Work]]** (The state of distraction-free concentration that creates high value; the antithesis of the fragmented attention economy).
    
- **Related-but-not-the-same:** **Advertising** (Traditional advertising tried to persuade a purchase; the Attention Economy tries to own the consciousness itself).

**Discernment:** Jesus identifies the "eye" (attention) as the gateway to the inner person. ## Notes

- **Pivot to Value:** As the Attention Economy saturates, "Silence" and "Focus" are becoming luxury goods. High-value users increasingly pay premium subscriptions specifically to remove ads and tracking mechanisms.
