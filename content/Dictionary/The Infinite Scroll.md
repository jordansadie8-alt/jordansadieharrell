# The Infinite Scroll

## Definition (1–2 sentences)
A UX design pattern where content loads continuously as the user scrolls down, eliminating page numbers or "stopping cues." It creates a loop of consumption that has no beginning, no end, and no "telos" (goal).

## Meaning (expanded)
Geometry matters. A book has a "bottom" (the end of the page). A movie has a "credits roll." These are stopping cues that allow the brain to reset and decide: "Am I done?" **The Infinite Scroll** removes the stopping cue. It is the geometry of the Abyss. It turns the user into a passive consumer of a stream that never runs dry, exploiting the brain's "unit bias" (the desire to finish what we start) against itself, because it is impossible to finish.

## Signs / markers
- **Time Blindness:** Intending to check for 5 minutes and losing 2 hours.
- **The "Thumb Twitch":** The automatic physical motion of scrolling even when you aren't looking at the screen or when the screen is locked.
- **Doomscrolling:** The inability to stop consuming negative information because the feed implies the "answer" or "safety" is just one scroll away.

## Examples (culture / tech / daily life)
- **TikTok/Reels:** The most aggressive form, where the video loops automatically, trapping the user in a double-infinity (vertical scroll + temporal loop).
- **Slot Machines:** The mechanical ancestor of the scroll—continuous play without breaks.

## Contrasts
- **Opposite:** [[Pagination]] (Breaking content into pages with a clear "Next" or "Stop" decision point).
- **Related-but-not-the-same:** [[Flow State]] (Flow is active engagement; Infinite Scroll is passive trance).
