## Definition
Historically, the writing of the lives of saints (from Greek *hagios*, "holy," + *graphia*, "writing") intended to edify believers rather than record historical fact. In modern usage, usually a pejorative term for a biography or account that idealizes its subject, removing all flaws, complexity, and nuance to create a perfect icon.

**Hagiography** began as a specific literary genre in the early Church (e.g., *The Golden Legend*). Its **Teleology** was not historical accuracy (what actually happened) but spiritual instruction (what *should* happen in a holy life). It used a "template" of holiness; the individual personality of the saint was often erased to fit the archetype of Christ.

In a **Modern/Secular context**, Hagiography refers to the "Cult of Personality." It is the mechanism of **Branding**. When we scrub a founder's history of their failures, or when an Instagram profile shows only the "wins," we are engaging in auto-hagiography.

Hagiography is the enemy of **Interiority**. Hagiography presents a smooth, impenetrable surface (the saint who never doubts). True Interiority (like St. Teresa’s) admits the chaos, the doubt, and the friction.

## Note
-   **Tech Connection:** "User Experience" (UX) design is often a form of hagiography; it smoothes out the friction of reality to create a "perfect" flow. Real life has friction.

## Signs / markers
-   **The "Smooth" Narrative:** A life story with no jagged edges, no catastrophic failures that aren't immediately redeemed.
-   **Projection of Archetype:** The subject ceases to be a human and becomes a symbol (e.g., "The Genius," "The Mother," "The Martyr").
-   **Suppression of Shadow:** The systematic omission of the subject's bad habits, cruelty, or doubts.

## Examples (culture / tech / daily life)
-   **The "Founder Myth":** Silicon Valley biographies (e.g., early Steve Jobs lore) that paint the founder as a solitary genius, omitting the collaborators, the luck, and the ruthless business tactics.
-   **Social Media Profiles:** The modern individual practices micro-hagiography daily—curating a feed that suggests a life of constant aesthetic peak experiences.
-   **Angélique in *The Dream*:** In Zola's novel, Angélique tries to live inside a Hagiography (*The Golden Legend*). It kills her because biological reality cannot sustain the perfection of the hagiographic ideal.

## Contrasts
-   **Opposite:** **Pathography:** (Joyce Carol Oates’ term) A biography that focuses entirely on the subject’s illness, failures, and sexual perversions (the "mud-raking" biography).
-   **Opposite:** **Critical Biography:** A historical account that weighs virtues and vices equally.
-   **Related-but-not-the-same:** **Panegyric:** A formal public speech of praise (hagiography is a written life story).

## Related terms

-   **Iconography** — The visual equivalent of hagiography.
-   **Propaganda** — Hagiography is the propaganda of the soul.
-   **[[Persona]]** — The mask constructed by hagiography.

## Biblical lens (NKJV)
**Scripture:**
* *"Elijah was a man with a nature like ours, and he prayed earnestly that it would not rain..."* — **James 5:17**
* *(The Bible generally rejects hagiography. It shows Noah drunk, David committing adultery, Peter denying Christ, and Paul persecuting the church.)*

**Discernment:**
The Bible uses **Realism**, not Hagiography. It argues that God works through *broken* vessels, not perfect ones. Hagiography suggests that you must be perfect to be used by God (which leads to despair/impostor syndrome). The Biblical lens suggests that your flaws are the very stage upon which Grace acts.
