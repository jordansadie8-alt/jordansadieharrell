## Definition  
A term coined by St. John of the Cross describing a crisis of spiritual transition where the believer feels completely abandoned by God, losing all sense of spiritual comfort, light, or pleasure. It is not a punishment, but a **passive purification** designed to strip the soul of its attachment to "spiritual candy" (feelings) so it can learn to love God for Himself alone.

**The Dark Night of the Soul** is the weaning process of the spirit. In the early stages of faith, God often grants "sensible consolations," warm feelings, easy prayers, and a clear sense of purpose, to encourage the beginner. But eventually, God withdraws these sensory props to force the soul to grow.

* **The Mechanism:** It acts like **Sensory Deprivation**. By darkening the intellect (it can't understand God) and drying up the will (it can't feel God), the soul is forced to operate by **Faith** alone.
* **The Logic:** You cannot unite with the Infinite if you are still addicted to the Finite. The Dark Night burns away the ego’s desire to "possess" spiritual experiences.
* **Psychological Shift:** It moves the center of identity from "I experience God" (Ego-centric spirituality) to "God experiences me" (Theo-centric spirituality).

## Archetypal Figures & Case Studies

> [!abstract] St. John of the Cross (The Prisoner Poet)
> **The Trait:** *Lyrical Endurance.*
> **The Analysis:** He wrote the poem *The Dark Night* while imprisoned in a tiny cell by his own religious order, beaten publicly, and starved. The "Dark Night" was literal before it was theological. He realized that the stripping of all human dignity was the necessary precursor to the "spiritual marriage."
> **Lesson:** The greatest light is often born in the smallest, darkest room.

> [!abstract] Mother Teresa (The Saint of Darkness)
> **The Trait:** *Fidelity in the Void.*
> **The Analysis:** Her private letters revealed she spent nearly 50 years—almost her entire ministry—in a state of profound spiritual darkness. She felt rejected by God, yet she worked with indefatigable love. She treated her darkness as a "share in the Passion of Christ."
> **Lesson:** Feeling God is not a requirement for serving God.

> [!abstract] Job (The Biblical Archetype)
> **The Trait:** *The Unjust Suffering.*
> **The Analysis:** Job loses everything—wealth, family, health—and his friends tell him it must be his fault. He sits on the ash heap, refusing to curse God but demanding an explanation. He is in the Dark Night because his relationship with God is shifting from a transactional contract ("I am good, You bless me") to a mystical encounter ("I have heard of You... but now my eye sees You").
> **Lesson:** The Night destroys your theology to save your faith.

> [!abstract] C.S. Lewis (A Grief Observed)
> **The Trait:** *The Intellectual Dark Night.*
> **The Analysis:** After his wife died, Lewis’s tidy apologetics collapsed. He described the silence of God as "a door slammed in your face." He had to rebuild his faith from the ground up, moving from a theory of God's goodness to a raw trust in it.
> **Lesson:** Intellect is no shield against the Night.

## The Narrative Arc (The Purgation)

### 1. The Night of Sense (The Dryness)
The beginner suddenly finds prayer boring and dry. The "old tricks" don't work. They feel like they are backsliding, but in reality, they are being moved to a new level. The senses are being quieted.

### 2. The Night of Spirit (The Crushing)
A deeper, more terrifying phase. The intellect, memory, and will are purged. The person feels rejected by God, perhaps even damned. They see their own sinfulness with blinding clarity.

### 3. The Dawn (The Integration)
The darkness does not necessarily "lift" like a curtain, but the eyes adjust. The soul realizes it is calm. It no longer needs "feelings" to be obedient. It enters **Mystical Union**.

## Signs / markers
-   **Dryness without Distraction:** You find no pleasure in the things of God (prayer/church), but you also find no pleasure in the things of the world. If you were just "backsliding," you would enjoy the world again. In the Dark Night, *nothing* tastes good.
-   **Anxiety of Loss:** A deep fear that you have lost the path or offended God, despite trying your best.
-   **Inability to Meditate:** The discursive mind (thinking in steps) jams. You can't "think" your way to God anymore.

## Examples 
-   **The "Valley of Despair" in Creativity:** The phase in a project where the initial excitement is gone, the result looks terrible, and you want to quit. The only way out is through.
-   **The "Void" in Grief:** The numbness that follows a tragedy, where the world feels paper-thin and God feels absent.
-   **Mid-Life Crisis:** Often a secular version of the Dark Night—the realization that the "first half" accomplishments (career, money) are empty, forcing a search for deeper meaning.

## Contrasts
-   **Opposite:** **Spiritual Gluttony:** Seeking God only for the "high" or the emotional rush (common in revivalist cultures).
-   **Opposite:** **Acedia (Sloth):** Being dry because you are lazy and negligent. In the Dark Night, you *want* to work but can't; in Acedia, you *could* work but don't want to.
-   **Related-but-not-the-same:** **Clinical Depression:** Depression is a pathology characterized by an inability to function, self-hatred, and chemical imbalance. The Dark Night is an *ontological* state where functionality often remains (Mother Teresa worked hard), but the *feeling* of connection is severed. (Note: They can overlap).

## Related terms
-   **Via Negativa** — (Apophatic Theology) The path of approaching God by denying what He is *not* (darkness/cloud) rather than affirming images.
-   **Desolation** — Ignatian term for the feeling of distance from God.
-   **[[Desert Fathers]]** — The original practitioners of this asceticism.

## Biblical lens (NKJV)
**Scripture:**
* *"My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?"* — **Psalm 22:1 / Matthew 27:46**
* *"I wait for the Lord, my soul waits, And in His word I do hope."* — **Psalm 130:5**

**Discernment:**
The supreme example of the Dark Night is **Jesus on the Cross**. At the moment of His greatest obedience (Union), He felt the greatest abandonment ("Why have You forsaken me?"). This validates the experience for every believer: feeling abandoned is not a sign of sin; it can be a sign of Sonship.
