## Definition 
A concept introduced by Julia Kristeva to describe the visceral reaction of horror and revulsion to that which threatens the boundaries of the "Self" (e.g., corpses, open wounds, bodily fluids). It is neither "Subject" (Me) nor "Object" (That), but the "Abject" (That which was me, but must be cast off for me to survive).

**Abjection** is the mechanism by which the human subject establishes identity by expelling what is "impure." To say "I am this," I must forcefully say "I am not that."

* **The Border Crisis:** The Abject is not merely "gross"; it is existentially dangerous because it blurs the line between the Self and the World. A corpse is the ultimate abject because it is a human body (Subject) that has become inanimate matter (Object), forcing the living viewer to confront their own eventual collapse into "meat."
* **The Primal Repression:** Psychologically, abjection begins in infancy. The child must "abject" the mother (rejecting the breast, the milk, the fusion) to become a separate entity. It is the violent separation required for autonomy.
* **The Collapse of Meaning:** The Abject does not respect rules, positions, or laws. It is the ambiguous, the composite, the in-between. It draws the subject toward the place where meaning collapses; a state of " fascination and repulsion."

## Archetypal Figures & Case Studies

> [!abstract] The Leper (Biblical/Historical)
> **The Trait:** *The Living Death.*
> **The Analysis:** The leper was historically treated as the embodiment of abjection. They were alive yet decaying (blurring life/death) and lived within the community yet were exiled from it (blurring inside/outside). The command to shout "Unclean!" was a ritualistic attempt to re-establish the border that the leper's body had violated.
> **Lesson:** Society maintains its coherence by designating and exiling the abject.

> [!abstract] The Alien (Xenomorph)
> **The Trait:** *Biological Invasion.*
> **The Analysis:** In the film *Alien*, the monster is terrifying not because it has a gun, but because it violates the body's integrity (oral impregnation, chest-bursting). It creates a "monstrous double," using the human body as raw material. It represents the fear that our biology is a trap.
> **Lesson:** Horror is the realization that the body is permeable.

> [!abstract] Lady Macbeth (Shakespeare)
> **The Trait:** *The Stain.*
> **The Analysis:** "Out, damned spot!" Lady Macbeth perceives a physical stain of blood that cannot be washed away. The moral guilt (internal) manifests as physical filth (external). She attempts to abject her own guilt through the ritual of washing, but because the abjection is spiritual, the physical ritual fails.
> **Lesson:** The "Stain" is often an internal reality projected onto the skin.

## Signs / markers
-   **Nausea:** The physiological refusal of the body to accept something. Gagging is the body saying, "This shall not enter."
-   **Fascination:** The inability to look away from a car crash or a wound. The Abject is magnetic because it touches the "Real"—the raw truth of existence before civilization sanitized it.
-   **Hyper-Cleanliness:** An obsessive need to sterilize the environment is often a defense mechanism against the fear of abjection (the fear that chaos will seep in).

## Examples 
-   **Food Waste:** A pristine apple is an "Object" of desire. A rotting apple, soft and leaking, is "Abject." It is the same matter, but it has lost its form and boundary.
-   **The "Uncanny Valley" in AI:** When a robot looks 99% human but moves slightly wrong, we feel revulsion. It threatens the border of "Human" vs. "Machine." It is an abject imitation of life.
-   **Hoarding:** The hoarder lives inside the abject. They refuse to "cast off" waste. The distinction between "Valuable Possession" and "Trash" dissolves, and the subject is eventually consumed by their own refuse.

## Contrasts
-   **Opposite:** **The Sublime:** The Sublime elevates the soul through awe (looking *up* at the mountains). The Abject drags the soul down through horror (looking *down* into the grave). Both are overwhelming, but in opposite directions.
-   **Opposite:** **The Object:** An object (like a table) is distinct, stable, and separate from me. The Abject is sticky, ambiguous, and threatens to merge with me.
-   **Related-but-not-the-same:** **The Uncanny:** The Uncanny is intellectual dread (something familiar becoming strange). Abjection is somatic dread (a physical reaction to the collapse of boundaries).

## Related terms
-   **[[Liminality]]** — The state of being "betwixt and between." The Abject always resides in liminal spaces (doorways, skin, twilight).
-   **[[Body Horror]]** — A genre entirely predicated on the fear of abjection.
-   **[[Taboo]]** — Cultural laws designed to manage the abject (dietary laws, burial rites).
-   **[[Entropy]]** — The physical force that turns structured systems into abject chaos.

## Biblical lens (NKJV)
**Scripture:**
* *"As a dog returns to his own vomit, So a fool repeats his folly."* — **Proverbs 26:11**
    * *Interpretation:* The image of consuming what was expelled (vomit) is the ultimate picture of degradation. It is the refusal to progress; it is a return to the undifferentiated mess.
* *"But I am a worm, and no man; A reproach of men, and despised by the people."* — **Psalm 22:6**
    * *Christological Abjection:* In the Passion, Christ becomes the Abject. He is stripped of human dignity ("no man"), bleeds, and is cast out of the city. He voluntarily enters the state of abjection to redeem it.
* *"Who will deliver me from this body of death?"* — **Romans 7:24**
    * *The Dualism:* Paul expresses the horror of the spiritual self being trapped in a biological vessel that is subject to decay and "lawlessness."

## Sources / lineage

## Notes
-   **Curatorial Note:** Abjection is necessary for life. Without a skin (border), we die. Without an immune system (rejection of the not-self), we die. The horror arises when these necessary borders fail.
-   **The Artistic Function:** Art is often a way of "purifying" the abject. By painting a wound or writing about a corpse (Catharsis), the artist gives *form* to the *formless*, allowing the viewer to contemplate the horror safely.
