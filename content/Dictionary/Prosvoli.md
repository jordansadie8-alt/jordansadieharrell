## Definition
From the Greek *prosvallō* ("to strike against" or "to assault"), **Prosvoli** is the initial, involuntary appearance of a thought, image, or idea in the consciousness. It is the "first knock" of temptation. Crucially, in Orthodox ascetic theology, **Prosvoli is sinless**. It is merely raw data—a mental reflex or external suggestion—before the human will has engaged with it.

**Prosvoli** is the "Notification" on the lock screen of the mind. It is information without ownership.
* **The No-Fault Zone:** A common spiritual error is to feel guilty for *having* a bad thought (e.g., a sudden blasphemy, a violent flash, or a sexual image). The Church Fathers teach that this is a mistake. You are not responsible for the birds flying over your head (Prosvoli), only for letting them build a nest in your hair (Consent).
* **The Mechanism:** It operates like a pop-up ad or a spam email. It is the brain's pattern-matching software offering a suggestion, or a demonic "arrow" fired from outside the fortress of the self.
* **The Tactic:** The correct response is not *argument* (which engages the thought) but *indifference* (which starves it). You simply "swipe left."

## The Narrative Arc (The Cognitive Chain)
Prosvoli is the first step in the linear progression of sin (or habit formation). Understanding where you are in this chain is the key to mental discipline.

1.  **Prosvoli (The Assault):** The thought appears. (Sinless). *Example: Seeing a drink.*
2.  **Sindyasmos (The Coupling):** The mind begins to "chat" with the thought. You analyze it or entertain the possibility. (Danger Zone). *Example: "One drink wouldn't hurt."*
3.  **Sygkatathesis (The Consent):** The will agrees to the thought. The decision is made, even if the action hasn't happened yet. (Sin Begins). *Example: "I will buy a bottle later."*
4.  **Aichmalosia (Captivity):** The thought takes over the mind; you are dragged along by it against your better judgment. *Example: Driving to the store on autopilot.*
5.  **Pathos (Passion):** The thought becomes a solidified habit or addiction. The fortress is occupied.

## Signs & Markers
* **Suddenness:** The thought appears out of nowhere, unrelated to the current context.
* **Alien Nature:** The thought feels "not like me" or ego-dystonic (e.g., a loving mother suddenly having a thought about dropping her baby). This is classic *Prosvoli*.
* **Repetition:** The thought buzzes like a fly; it is annoying rather than enticing at first.

## Examples
* **The Glitch:** You are driving a car and suddenly think, "I could swerve into oncoming traffic." This is not a suicidal desire; it is a Prosvoli (an intrusive thought checking the system's limits).
* **The Flash:** Seeing an attractive person and registering their beauty before any lustful desire forms. The optic nerve has fired (Prosvoli), but the will has not yet corrupted the data.
* **The Spam Filter:** Your email inbox receives a scam. You see it (Prosvoli), but you do not click it (Consent). You remain safe.

## Contrasts
* **Opposite: [[Intentionality]]** — Prosvoli is uninvited; Intention is invited.
* **Opposite: [[Consent]]** — The moment you say "Yes" to the image.
* **Nuance: [[Impulse]]** — An impulse often involves a bodily urge (hunger, adrenaline); Prosvoli is specifically a *cognitive* image or suggestion.

## Biblical Lens
**Scripture:**
* *"The prince of this world comes, and has nothing in Me."* — **John 14:30**
    * *Exegesis:* Jesus experienced the *Prosvoli* of the enemy (the temptation in the desert), but the thoughts found no "hooks" or "purchase" in Him. The arrows struck the shield and fell off.
* *"Above all, taking the shield of faith with which you will be able to quench all the fiery darts of the wicked one."* — **Ephesians 6:16**
    * *Exegesis:* The "fiery darts" are *Prosvoli*. The shield stops them before they burn the flesh (the heart).

## Notes
* **The "2-Second Rule":** You generally have about 2 seconds after a Prosvoli hits to reject it before it turns into *Sindyasmos* (Conversation).
* **Technique:** Do not say "I must not think about the white bear." Instead, look at the white bear, label it "Bear," and return to your work.
