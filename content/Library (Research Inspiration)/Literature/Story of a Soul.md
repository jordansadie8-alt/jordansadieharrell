# Story of a Soul

## Why I saved this
- **The "Micro-Teleology":** Thérèse invented the "Little Way"—finding the ultimate teleology in the smallest, most invisible actions (folding laundry, smiling at someone you hate).
- **The Psychology of Need:** She reframes "weakness" and "neediness" not as flaws, but as the **"Interface for Grace."** The smaller you are, the more God fills you.
- **Interior Steel:** Beneath the "flowery" language is a will of iron. She is a "Strong Woman" who conquered the self completely.

## Key themes
- **The Little Way:** Great holiness through ordinary things.
- **Spiritual Childhood:** Radical trust, bypassing the complex "adult" interface of intellectual theology.
- **The Elevator:** She compares God's grace to an elevator (a modern invention at her time)—she is too small to climb the stairs, so He must lift her.

## Key quotes
- "I will spend my heaven doing good on earth."
- "Miss no single opportunity of making some small sacrifice, here by a smiling look, there by a kindly word."
- "To pick up a pin for love can convert a soul."

## Notes / takeaways
- **Framing Connection:** Thérèse frames her life as a "romance" or a "flower garden," but the reality was tuberculosis, depression, and darkness. The *frame* kept her sane.
- **Psychological Insight:** A study in **Sublimation**. She sublimated every irritation and pain into an act of love.
- **Feminine Breakdown:** Her "trial of faith" at the end of her life—she stared into the void of atheism and chose to believe anyway.
