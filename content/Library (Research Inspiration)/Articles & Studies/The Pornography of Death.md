# [The Pornography of Death (View PDF)](https://www.romolocapuano.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/08/Gorer.pdf)

## Why I saved this
- **The Teleology of Taboo:** To understand Gorer's breakthrough thesis: that "prudery" is a movable energy. It does not disappear; it shifts. As society became open about sex, it became repressed about death.
- **The Interface of Grief:** To analyze how the loss of public rituals (mourning clothes, deathbed gatherings) has forced the human need for death-processing into a "private, hallucinatory" interface (horror comics, violent movies); which Gorer defines as "pornographic."
- **The "Hydraulic" Theory of Repression:** The argument that if you suppress the "natural" expression of a universal drive (natural death/grief), it will inevitably burst out in a distorted, "unnatural" form (obsession with violent death).

## Key themes
- **The Great Inversion:** In the 19th Century, Sex was the "Unspeakable" and Death was the "Public Spectacle" (cemeteries, deathbeds, executions). In the 20th Century, this flipped entirely. Copulation is chatty; Death is hidden in hospitals and euphemisms ("passed on").
- **Definition of Pornography:** Gorer defines pornography not by content, but by *function*. It is "hallucinatory fantasy" used as a substitute for real experience. Therefore, a detective novel about murder is functionally identical to a "dirty book"; both provide a safe, unrealistic thrill for an instinct that is blocked in real life.
- **The Denial of Corruption:** The modern refusal to acknowledge physical decay. The "cosmetic" treatment of the corpse is an attempt to deny the reality of the biological end, just as Victorians denied the reality of the biological beginning (stork/gooseberry bush).
- **The Theology of the Body:** Gorer links the ability to face death with a belief in the soul. When the body was seen as a "seed" for resurrection (Christianity), corruption could be faced. When the body is seen as the *only* reality (Secularism), its end becomes too terrifying to mention.

## Key quotes
- "The natural processes of corruption and decay have become disgusting, as disgusting as the natural processes of birth and copulation were a century ago."
- "There is no valid evidence to suppose that either type of pornography is an incitement to action; rather are they substitute gratifications."
- "If we make death unmentionable in polite society, 'not before the children', we almost ensure the continuation of the 'horror comic.' No censorship has ever been really effective."

## Notes / takeaways
- **Framing Connection:** Gorer reframes "Horror" not as a genre of entertainment, but as a symptom of **Repression**. We consume violence because we are starved of the reality of mortality.
- **Sociological Insight:** The shift from "Community Mourning" (a shared social event) to "Private Grief" (a shameful secret). He argues that this privatization isolates the individual, leading to mental health crises that didn't exist when mourning was a public duty.
- **Anthropological Lens:** Gorer treats 1950s England/America like a Trobriand island. He observes the "Rules of Seemliness"; the specific words you cannot say in front of specific people. He identifies "Death" as the new "Four Letter Word."

## Analysis & Archetypes
**The Core Argument:**
Gorer argues that humans have an innate need to interface with the three absolutes: Birth, Copulation, Death. If a society blocks the "Front Door" (Ritual/Religion/Community) to any of these, the human psyche breaks in through the "Back Door" (Pornography/Fantasy/Obsession).

**Archetypes & Embodiments:**
- **Archetype:** *The Victorian Prude vs. The Modern Prude.* The Victorian fainted at the sight of an ankle but picnicked in a cemetery. The Modern discusses orgasms at lunch but lies to children about where Grandma went.
- **Archetype:** *The Embalmer.* The priest of the new religion of Denial, whose job is to make the dead look "as if they are sleeping."
- **Real World Application:** *The True Crime Epidemic.* Our massive consumption of podcasts/documentaries about murder is the direct result of our lack of contact with natural death. We are death-starved, so we binge on the "pornography" of homicide.
